# Recycling findings (checked 2026-11-16)

## Method
One consistent metric for all 12 materials: The Recycling Partnership (TRP) State of Recycling 2024 national residential recycling rate per material. It counts every loss: household access, material acceptance, participation, putting it in the bin, and the sorting facility selling it. Only source that measures what happens at a US home and treats paper and film the same way. Data pulled October 2023 (recorded as data year 2023).

## Recommended recycle_rate
corrugated 0.32 (cardboard, direct) | kraft-paper 0.23 (mixed paper, proxy) | paperboard 0.23 (proxy) | molded-pulp 0.23 (proxy, likely too high) | paper-plastic-mix 0.0 (estimate) | pe-film 0.01 (top of "<1%") | pp-film 0.0 (estimate) | plastic-laminate 0.0 (estimate) | eps-foam 0.01 (film treatment, proxy) | glass 0.27 (direct) | aluminum 0.30 (cans; likely too high for tins) | steel 0.19 (cans, direct)

## Why the paper mailer lost before
EPA's 20.8% paper figure is mostly folding cartons and covers businesses too, but lands close to TRP mixed paper 23%. The real problem was film's 10% (EPA 2018, businesses plus homes, all film types). TRP residential film is under 1% (0.1% in state table). New gap: 0.77 vs 0.99 of weight unrecycled. Paper still loses on the weight-based waste score if the paper mailer weighs more than ~1.29x the plastic one.

## Corrugated: 96.5% vs 70-75% vs 32%
- EPA 96.5% (2018): older counting style AF&PA itself replaced.
- AF&PA changed its formula with 2023 data (old method gave 93.6% for 2022; missed imported boxes, counted scrap, contaminants and moisture). 2022 recalculated and 2025: 70-75%. Covers industrial, commercial, institutional and residential together.
- TRP residential 32%: households that recycle put 81% of cardboard in the bin; facilities sell 90%; access and participation pull it to 32%.
- Sensitivity: EPA August 2024 report, residential 2019 cardboard 53.5% (state-reported); no film figure.

## Recycled content defaults (estimates)
corrugated 0.52 (Fibre Box Association 2025 via Packaging Dive, secondary) | aluminum 0.71 (Aluminum Association and CMI 2022, beverage cans, primary) | steel 0.25 (2012 Waste360 citing industry, a minimum, secondary) | glass none (GPI says "typically up to 70%", a ceiling) | not found: kraft mailers, molded pulp, paperboard, poly mailers.

## Mailer guidance
- All-paper padded mailers: AF&PA says they "can go in your curbside bin"; mills with 85% of mixed-paper use and 83% of cardboard use accept them.
- Bubble-lined paper mailers: AF&PA says "separate the paper layer from the bubble wrap lining"; recycle only the paper.
- How2Recycle: plastic mailers need the paper label removed before store drop-off. Only pure PE film is accepted at drop-off.

## Conflicts
- Glass, aluminum, steel run 9-12 points below EPA 2019 residential figures (TRP models from local program data; EPA from state reports).
- Aluminum cans 30% (TRP) vs 43% (industry, includes deposit returns).
- EPS: industry says 31% in North America 2022, "primarily" business-to-business, not curbside.

## Could not confirm
Whether TRP film rate includes store drop-off (hence 0.01 not 0.001); whether TRP mixed paper includes paperboard or kraft mailers; whether TRP glass includes deposit returns; molded pulp national data (0.23 likely high); aluminum tins and lids (0.30 likely high); single data year; 2026 TRP update material rates; EPA 2018 figures not reopened.
