# Right-sizing, void fill and reuse findings (checked 2026-11-16)

No empty-space figure comes from a peer-reviewed or government measurement; the only peer-reviewed number is in reuse.

## EU rule (primary): Regulation (EU) 2025/40, Article 24
- Empty space ratio at most 50% for grouped, transport and e-commerce packaging.
- Ratio = (outer volume - volume of the product in its own sales packaging) / outer volume.
- Filler counts as empty space: "paper cuttings, air cushions, bubble wraps, sponge fillers, foam fillers, wood wool, polystyrene or Styrofoam chips". Only a smaller box fixes the ratio.
- Applies from 1 January 2030, or 3 years after the Commission's calculation rules enter into force (due by 12 Feb 2028), whichever is later. Regulation applies generally from 12 Aug 2026.
- Exempt: products shipped in own sales packaging; reusable packaging in a re-use system.
- Read from an Official Journal PDF copy (EUR-Lex would not render); cite EUR-Lex.

## Empty space (all secondary)
- ~70% air, ~30% item (Fraunhofer IML, 2024 press article, no method).
- 40% empty on average (Packsize, box-machine vendor, 2013).
- About a third of retailer parcels at least double the product size (DS Smith, box vendor, 2018); such boxes are at least 50% empty (our arithmetic).
- Up to 64% air in some categories (Paccurate, software vendor, undated).
Present as a range of industry estimates; use the EU 50% limit as the yardstick.

## Right-sizing results (company-reported)
- Amazon: 43% lower packaging weight per shipment 2015-2023 (2023 report); 5.4 million metric tons avoided since 2015; 11% of packages shipped with no added Amazon packaging in 2025.
- Walmart (2016): box options 12 to 27; cardboard use >30% more efficient across >10,000 test orders; three truckloads became two.
- Paccurate: boxes 14% smaller on average, about 1 sq ft cardboard saved per carton.

## Reuse
- Peer-reviewed (Zimmermann and Hauschke, Sustainability, 2024): reusable plastic box beats single-use cardboard box on carbon after 10-12 uses; reusable bag beats cardboard box after 1 use, paper bag after 8-9.
- WWF-Singapore 2022 pilot: ~50% return over 15,000 orders; 75-83% needed for economics.
- Company-reported: Boox breaks even on first use assuming 12 uses and 85% return (2020); RePack 57-82% lower carbon per cycle vs plastic mailer, assuming 95% return ("aspirational").
- EU: 40% of transport packaging incl. e-commerce must be reusable from 2030 (cardboard boxes excluded). No US state reuse fee discount found beyond Oregon Bonus C (from fee research).

## Void fill density
- Crinkle paper: Uline Monster Crinkle 10 lb fills 3.5 cu ft = 1,296 g/cu ft; EcoEnclose shred 11 lb fills 13,068 cu in = ~660 g/cu ft. Brackets our 907 estimate. Crinkle is dense decorative fill, not efficient void fill.
- Crumpled kraft: our 60 g/cu ft is an assumption, probably a low bound. Pregis (sells both systems, secondary): air cushions weigh about one fifth of paper pads for the same volume, implying ~300 g/cu ft for machine paper pads.

## Conflicts
- Amazon tonnage: 3 Mt (2023 report) vs 5.4 Mt (current page); use 5.4 Mt "since 2015".
- Empty space averages range 40% to ~70%.
- Crumpled kraft 60 vs ~300 g/cu ft; weigh it.

## Could not confirm
Peer-reviewed or government average empty space or void fill per package; peer-reviewed right-sizing savings; paper per cu ft from void-fill machines (spec sheets give feed rates only); independent densities for air pillows and peanuts; LimeLoop, Returnity, Olive studies; numeric break-even from TemaNord 2024:515; US state EPR treatment of reuse beyond Oregon.
